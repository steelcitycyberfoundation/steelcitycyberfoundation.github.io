Password Security

By Elias Z. · Steel City Cyber Foundation

Here's the honest truth: if you're using the same password for everything, you're one leaked website away from losing a bunch of accounts at once. It's the most common mistake we see, and it's completely fixable.

Why your old advice is wrong

You've probably heard "use a complex password with symbols and numbers." That advice is outdated. The real problem isn't how complex your password is — it's that you reuse it.

Hackers don't guess your password one by one. They steal a whole database from one website, then try those same email and password combos everywhere else. It's called credential stuffing, and it works because most people reuse passwords.

What actually works

Use a password manager. It generates a long, unique password for every site and remembers them all for you. You only need to remember one master password. That's it.

Good free options:

  • Bitwarden — free, open source, works on phone and computer
  • KeePassXC — fully offline, if you don't want your passwords in the cloud
  • Built-in ones on your phone (iCloud Keychain, Google Password Manager) — better than nothing

Make the master password count

The one password you do need to remember should be a passphrase — a few random words strung together. Long beats complex.

Good: blue-owl-walnut-tunnel

Bad: P@ssw0rd1! (looks strong, but it's on every hacker's list)

Don't use your dog's name, your street, or your birthday. Those are public information to anyone who knows you or looks at your social media.

When a password leaks

Sites get breached all the time. Check yourself at haveibeenpwned.com — it tells you if your email has shown up in a data breach. It's free and safe to use.

If you find out you're in a breach, change that password now. If you reused it, change it everywhere.

Quick checklist

  • Set up a password manager
  • Let it generate unique passwords for every account
  • Make your master password a long passphrase, not a word
  • Check your email on haveibeenpwned.com
  • Turn on two-factor authentication (see our device security guide)

That's the whole thing. One hour of setup and you're better protected than most people.
