CTFs for Beginners

By Elias Z. · Steel City Cyber Foundation

A CTF (Capture The Flag) is a hacking competition. Organizers set up intentionally vulnerable targets — websites, files, networks — and your job is to find hidden "flags" by exploiting them. A flag is usually a string of text like flag{you_got_me}. Find it, submit it, get points.

It sounds intimidating. It's actually the most fun way to learn security, and you don't need to be an expert to start.

Why it's safe and legal

This is the part that scares people, so let's get it out of the way. In a CTF, you attack practice targets that were built to be attacked. That's the difference between a CTF and hacking a real website — one gets you a job, the other gets you in trouble. Stick to CTF platforms and you're completely fine.

The common challenge types

  • Web: find flaws in a website — broken logins, hidden pages, code you can mess with.
  • Cryptography: decode ciphers and figure out badly-implemented encryption.
  • Forensics: dig through files, images, and memory dumps for hidden info.
  • OSINT: find information that's publicly available but hard to find. Basically detective work.
  • Reverse engineering: take apart a program to figure out what it does.
  • Pwn: exploit a program's bug to take control. The hardest category, and usually a later step.

Start with web, crypto, forensics, and OSINT. They're the most approachable.

Where to start (all free)

  • picoCTF — the best first CTF. Made by Carnegie Mellon for beginners, with built-in lessons. Start here, seriously.
  • OverTheWire Bandit — teaches Linux and command line by playing. Great if you're new to the terminal (see our command line guide).
  • TryHackMe — guided, browser-based rooms. Free tier is plenty to start.
  • CTFtime — the calendar of upcoming CTF competitions. Try a beginner-friendly team one when you're ready.

Tools you'll meet (don't panic)

You'll hear about tools like Burp Suite (web proxy), Wireshark (network capture), and CyberChef (data decoding). You don't need any of them on day one. picoCTF has everything you need in your browser. Learn one thing at a time as the challenges demand it.

How to not get overwhelmed

  • Play solo or with one friend first. Big team CTFs are chaos when you're new.
  • Don't try to solve everything. Pick one category and stick to it.
  • It's normal to spend hours on one challenge. That's how you learn — the aha moment is the point.
  • Read the writeups after the event. People explain how they solved each challenge. You learn more from those than from solving.

Quick checklist

  • Make a picoCTF account
  • Do the beginner lessons, then the easiest challenges
  • Try OverTheWire Bandit if the terminal is new to you
  • Stick to one category at first
  • Read writeups after events — that's where the learning is

Everyone who's good at this was once staring at a challenge with no idea where to start. The only difference is they kept going.
