Device Security

By Elias Z. · Steel City Cyber Foundation

You don't need to be a security pro to lock down your devices. Four things cover 90% of the risk: updates, two-factor auth, a lock screen, and a sane home network.

1. Turn on automatic updates

Updates aren't Microsoft and Apple being annoying. They patch holes that attackers actively use. When a vulnerability is found, it's public knowledge — and criminals race to exploit it on devices that haven't updated.

Your phone and computer both have an "automatic updates" setting. Turn it on. That's the entire step. If a notification says "Restart to finish updating," just do it. It takes two minutes.

2. Two-factor authentication (2FA)

2FA means that even if someone gets your password, they still can't get in — because they'd need a second thing: a code from your phone.

Turn it on for the accounts that matter most, in this order:

  1. Your email (this is the master key — it's how every other account resets its password)
  2. Your bank
  3. Social media

Use an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, or the built-in one on your phone) instead of text message codes. Texts can be intercepted by someone who steals your SIM. An app is a code only you can see.

3. Lock screen and find-my-device

Set a PIN or password that locks your phone after a minute or two of no use. Face ID and fingerprint unlock are fine — they're the lock, not the only lock.

Turn on Find My iPhone / Find My Device. If your phone gets stolen, you can lock it remotely, wipe it, or track it. Most people set this up once and forget it — and it's the reason stolen phones are useless to thieves.

4. Home Wi-Fi

Your router is the front door to every device in your house. Most people never touch the settings.

  • Change the router password from the default. "admin/admin" is on every hacker's list.
  • Use WPA2 or WPA3 encryption (it's a dropdown in your router settings).
  • Make a separate "guest" network for smart home stuff and visitors. That way, a compromised smart bulb can't reach your laptop.

To reach your router settings, open a browser and go to something like 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1 — check the sticker on the router for the exact address and default login.

Quick checklist

  • Automatic updates on for phone and computer
  • 2FA on email, bank, and social media — using an app, not texts
  • Screen locks after inactivity, find-my-device enabled
  • Router password changed, WPA2/WPA3 enabled
  • Guest Wi-Fi for smart home devices

Do these once and you're ahead of most people — and way ahead of most attackers' targets.
