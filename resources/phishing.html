Phishing & Scams

By Elias Z. · Steel City Cyber Foundation

Phishing is when someone pretends to be a company (or a person) you trust, so you hand over your password, money, or personal info. It's the #1 way accounts get hacked — not because it's clever, but because it works.

The red flags

Scammers rush you. That's the whole game. Legitimate companies don't need you to act in the next 15 minutes.

  • Urgency: "Your account will be locked in 24 hours!"
  • Fear: "Suspicious activity detected on your account."
  • Rewards: "You've won a gift card. Claim it now."
  • Bad grammar: Real companies proofread. Scammers don't.
  • Weird sender: support@amaz0n-verify.com is not Amazon.

The fake login page trick

Here's the sneaky one. The email looks fine, so you click the link. The page looks exactly like your bank's login. You type your password. Congratulations — you just gave it to the scammer.

The fix: don't click links in emails to reach your accounts. Open a new tab and type the website yourself. Bookmark it. That's the only way to be sure you're on the real site.

It's not just email

  • Texts (smishing): "Your package is stuck. Track it here:" with a link. UPS doesn't text you from a random number.
  • Calls (vishing): Someone says they're from Microsoft and your computer has a virus. No — that's a scam. Real tech support never calls you out of the blue.
  • Fake invoices: An emailed "invoice" for something you never ordered, with a "contact us to cancel" number. Calling that number is the trap.
  • Family emergency: "Grandma, I'm in jail, send money" — always verify with a phone call to the actual person.

You clicked. Now what?

Don't panic. Do these in order:

  1. Change the password on the account you entered (if you entered one)
  2. If you reused it, change it everywhere it's used
  3. Turn on two-factor auth if you haven't (see our device security guide)
  4. If you typed a credit card number, call the bank and report it

Quick checklist

  • Slow down. Real companies don't rush you.
  • Never click login links in emails — type the address yourself
  • Never call numbers from a suspicious email or invoice
  • Verify family emergencies with a real phone call
  • Report phishing emails to your email provider (most have a "Report phishing" button)

When in doubt, don't click. A scammer's whole job is to make you feel like you don't have time to think.
