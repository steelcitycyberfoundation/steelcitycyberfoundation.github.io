Privacy Online

By Elias Z. · Steel City Cyber Foundation

Privacy isn't about hiding from the government. It's about deciding who gets your info — instead of letting every website and app take it by default.

What's tracking you

When you browse the web, companies drop little trackers in your browser. They build a profile of what you look at, what you click, and where you go. That profile gets sold to advertisers. It also gets stolen in data breaches.

The fix is mostly in your browser settings — not apps you have to pay for.

Browser settings that matter

  • Turn on "Do Not Track" and tracking protection. Firefox has it on by default (Enhanced Tracking Protection). Safari and Edge have similar settings.
  • Install a content blocker. uBlock Origin is free, open source, and blocks most trackers and ads. It makes the web faster too.
  • Use a private window for sensitive stuff. It doesn't make you anonymous, but it doesn't save your history or cookies. Good for shared computers.
  • Use a search engine that doesn't track you. DuckDuckGo or Startpage instead of Google. Same results, less profile-building.

The "sign in with Google" button

Every site offers "Sign in with Google" because it's convenient. But it also connects that site's activity to your Google account — one big profile of everything you do.

You don't need to stop using it forever. But for anything you care about (banking, health, work), make a separate account with its own password. It's worth the two extra minutes.

Your phone knows a lot

  • Check app permissions. Why does a flashlight app need your contacts and location? It doesn't. On iPhone: Settings → Privacy. On Android: Settings → Apps → each app's permissions. Revoke anything that doesn't make sense.
  • Location history: Most phones track everywhere you go. You can turn this off or delete the history (iPhone: Settings → Privacy → Location Services → System Services → Significant Locations; Android: Google account settings → Data & privacy).
  • Turn off ad personalization. Settings → Privacy → Advertising. It doesn't stop ads, it stops them being built from your data.

The quiz problem

"Which Pixar character are you?" quizzes aren't harmless. They ask your name, pet, hometown, birthday — the exact answers to security questions. Dozens of them, combined, are a profile of your identity.

Skip quizzes that ask personal details. If a site asks security questions, treat the answers like passwords (make them up, write them in your password manager).

Quick checklist

  • Turn on tracking protection in your browser
  • Install uBlock Origin
  • Use a private search engine
  • Audit app permissions on your phone
  • Turn off location history and ad personalization
  • Stop doing personality quizzes with real info

Privacy is a bunch of small defaults, not one big action. Change the defaults and you're done.
